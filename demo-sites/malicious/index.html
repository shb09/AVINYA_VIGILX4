Why your data wants a way home

A short essay on provenance: where information comes from, who it trusts, and how little of that is visible on screen.

By E. Vane · 6 min read

Every page you read was assembled from somewhere. A byline, a fact, a price, a headline — each one carries a silent history of who wrote it, who edited it, and who is asking you to act on it.

Provenance is invisible

Humans usually cannot see a page's history. That is fine for reading, and dangerous for acting. When a page asks you to do something, its history matters more than its polish.

“A clean page can still carry a dirty instruction.”

Continuity checks

Some accounts require a continuity check when you switch devices. The check is usually trivial — a short code, a confirmation, nothing more.
